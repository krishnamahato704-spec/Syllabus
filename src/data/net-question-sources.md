# NET practice bank: coverage and source notes

Reviewed 30 October 2026. This is an authored practice bank, not a transcription of official past papers, an official answer key or an official difficulty classification. The difficulty labels are author judgements. Topic references identify relevant official or scholarly material; they do not imply that a linked source contains the question or certifies its answer.

There are 220 distinct questions: 80 Paper I questions, eight in each of its ten units; and 140 History questions, fourteen in each of its ten units. The 20 Paper I and 40 History topics in the old banks were retained in reviewed adaptations. The remaining 160 questions are new. Stems were rewritten when necessary to remove overclaims, ambiguities and unsupported language. No old generator is imported.

Paper I includes two original prose passages with four questions each, eight mathematical reasoning questions, eight logical reasoning questions and eight questions on two self-contained original data tables. The numerical explanations show the calculation or state the limit of the available data. A table appears in each DI stem so that its question remains answerable when a paper selects an individual item. The passages and data are written for this app. They are not extracts from the linked syllabus.

Unit 3 is **early medieval**, not ancient in a strict chronological sense. The `ancient` slug groups the pre-Sultanate/early medieval survey for compatibility with the app’s existing four period domains. Any UI using all three units should label it **Ancient and early medieval**. A strictly ancient drill should select only `history-1` and `history-2`. The bank contains no Mughal, Sultanate or British subject matter in those first three units. Later-period names may occur as distractors. This grouping does not redefine the official unit boundaries.

Paper I uses the same domain and unit slugs: teaching, research, comprehension, communication, mathematical-reasoning, logical-reasoning, data-interpretation, ict, environment and higher-education. Every item has a leaf ID checked against the existing `syllabus.json`. These are app checklist IDs, not identifiers assigned by NTA.

Balanced History papers are an app practice choice. There is no claim that NTA prescribes a fixed ten-question quota for each History unit. Fourteen available items per unit allow a 100-question balanced practice paper to draw ten per unit without repeating an item within that paper. Separate generated papers must overlap because the pool is finite. The bank covers selected topics in every unit; it does not claim exhaustive topic coverage or psychometric validation.

## Sources and verification

The [official UGC NET syllabus index](https://www.ugcnetonline.in/syllabus-new.php) was read and confirms the Paper I and History syllabus links. Direct legacy PDF paths now returned directory listings in one retrieval tool; the download wrapper redirected to short-lived S3 URLs that another tool could not fetch. The stable index is used in the data instead of a signed expiring URL. The existing local syllabus supplied the detailed unit boundaries and the checklist IDs.

History topic references primarily use the official [NIOS History 315 collection](https://rcbengaluru.nios.ac.in/history-315.html) and its chapter PDFs. The text of lessons 2–7, 9–16 and 18–21 was retrieved in this review. Lesson 8 and lesson 17 links were confirmed in the official collection, but their PDF text timed out in repeated retrieval attempts; their presence is not described as a successful full-text check. NIOS is a topic reference at school level, not a claim that its chapters alone provide postgraduate depth.

Additional references include the [Fitzwilliam Museum’s Rabatak research record](https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/publication/publication-200002496), the [University of Michigan/ACLS record for Burton Stein’s monograph](https://www.fulcrum.org/concern/monographs/gf06g2796), [John R. Hall’s Annales article abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781405165518.wbeosa053), the Stanford Encyclopedia entries for [Indian logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-india/), [Foucault](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/foucault/) and [Collingwood](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/collingwood/), and the [University of Waterloo record on Orientalism](https://uwaterloo.ca/equity-diversity-inclusion-anti-racism/catalogs/educational-catalogue-resources/1978-orientalism-edward-w-said). The Stein record is a bibliographic/topic reference; its full text required institutional access.

The IGNOU/eGyanKosh records for historical method, research approaches, Subaltern Studies and Indian historiography were located through indexed official records. Several direct full-text/handle retrievals timed out or returned gateway errors, so those references must not be represented as complete full-text source checks. The IGNCA/ASI Burzahom PDF was indexed with the correct title, but direct text retrieval also timed out. The NCERT political science book link is an official topic reference; direct NCERT chapter text was not retrievable during this review. Ministry of Education’s NEP landing page resolved but exposed no useful text to the retrieval tool. The current page should be checked before adding detailed new policy questions.

Paper I sources successfully retrieved include [Carnegie Mellon’s assessment guide](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/formative-summative.html), [UIC’s Bloom taxonomy guide](https://teaching.uic.edu/cate-teaching-guides/syllabus-course-design/blooms-taxonomy-of-educational-objectives/), [SWAYAM](https://swayam.gov.in/about), [ORI’s plagiarism guidance](https://ori.hhs.gov/plagiarism), the [UGC institutional page](https://www.ugc.gov.in/), the [National Portal’s NAAC description](https://www.india.gov.in/category/education-learning/subcategory/higher-education/details/website-of-national-assessment-and-accreditation-council), [UN SDG 4](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal4), [UNEP’s Montreal Protocol page](https://www.unep.org/ozonaction/who-we-are/about-montreal-protocol), [EPA’s greenhouse gas overview](https://www.epa.gov/ghgemissions/overview-greenhouse-gases), and the [IETF TLS 1.3 specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8446). TLS is used only to support the stable encrypted-transport concept, not a claim that this older RFC is the latest standard.

## Corrections made while adapting

- Lothal’s basin is described as the excavators’ dockyard interpretation, not an undisputed identification.
- CPU cache is the fastest **among the listed options**; registers are not incorrectly ruled out by a universal claim.
- SF₆ is compared by 100-year GWP **per unit mass** and only against the gases listed. No unsupported current numerical GWP is given.
- Takshashila is located without asserting that a list of ancient authors all taught there, or treating it as a modern university.
- Barani’s chronicle is identified through its coverage, since Afif also wrote a Tarikh-i Firoz Shahi.
- Awadh’s annexation on alleged misgovernment is distinguished from the Doctrine of Lapse.
- The Permanent Settlement fixes the state demand on zamindars, not all tenant rents. The old question about the settlement’s purported principal designers was replaced by this clearer institutional distinction.
- The Ghadar movement is not attributed to a single founder. Its leaders Bhakna and Har Dayal are named together.
- External criticism is not equated with heuristics; heuristics means locating sources. No universal Mughal tax share is asserted in the Irfan Habib item.

## Checks

Run `node --test tests/net-content.test.mjs`. The checks cover 220 unique IDs/stems, four non-duplicate options and valid keys, all twenty unit pools, domain mapping, known local syllabus leaves, consistent reading passages, embedded tables and independently worked numerical answers. They also preserve selected historical distinctions corrected above. These checks do not replace further subject-specialist review of every distractor or establish official question difficulty.
